## 1. Estructura de una presentación formal

### 1.1 Apertura

```text
Good morning, everyone. Thank you for being here today.
I'm delighted to have the opportunity to speak with you about...
Today, I'll be discussing three main points: first... second... and finally...
```

### 1.2 Cuerpo: transiciones entre puntos

| Expresión | Uso |
|---|---|
| Let's move on to... | Pasemos a... |
| Turning to the next point... | Pasando al siguiente punto... |
| As I mentioned earlier... | Como mencioné antes... |
| This brings me to my next point. | Esto me lleva a mi siguiente punto. |
| To illustrate this, consider... | Para ilustrar esto, consideren... |

### 1.3 Cierre

```text
To summarize the key points I've discussed today...
In closing, I'd like to emphasize that...
Thank you for your time and attention. I'd be happy to take any
questions.
```

## 2. Manejando preguntas del público

### 2.1 Ganar tiempo para pensar

| Expresión | Uso |
|---|---|
| That's a great question. | Esa es una excelente pregunta. |
| Let me think about that for a moment. | Déjenme pensar eso un momento. |
| I'm glad you asked that. | Me alegra que preguntes eso. |

### 2.2 Cuando no sabes la respuesta

```text
That's an excellent point, and I don't have the exact figures with
me right now, but I'd be happy to follow up with you after the
presentation.

I'm not entirely sure about that specific detail, but I can look
into it and get back to you.
```

**Nota importante:** admitir que no sabes algo, de forma profesional y ofreciendo seguimiento, es mucho más creíble y respetado que inventar una respuesta insegura — esto conecta con lo que aprendiste sobre honestidad y hedging.

## 3. Vocabulario y expresiones de apoyo visual (útil si presentas con diapositivas)

| Expresión | Uso |
|---|---|
| As you can see on this slide... | Como pueden ver en esta diapositiva... |
| This chart illustrates... | Este gráfico ilustra... |
| Let's take a closer look at... | Veamos más de cerca... |

## 4. Ejemplo en contexto: presentación completa (fragmento)

```text
Good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for joining me today. I'm
excited to share our findings on customer satisfaction trends.

Today, I'll cover three areas: our methodology, key findings, and
recommendations for next steps.

Let's start with our methodology. We surveyed over 500 customers
across three regions.

As you can see on this slide, satisfaction scores increased by
15% compared to last year. This brings me to my next point:
what drove this improvement?

Turning to our recommendations, we believe expanding our customer
support team would further enhance these results.

To summarize, our data clearly shows positive momentum, and we
have a clear path forward. Thank you for your attention. I'd be
happy to take any questions.

[Question from audience]

That's a great question. I don't have that specific breakdown with
me right now, but I'll follow up with you by email this week.
```

**No traducimos este fragmento completo** — léelo con calma, identificando la estructura de apertura, transiciones, cierre y manejo de preguntas.

## 7. 🧠 Retrieval practice

### Ejercicio 1 — Completa la estructura de la presentación

1. ___, everyone. Thank you for ___ ___ today. (apertura)
2. ___ ___ our next point. (turning to)
3. ___ ___ the key points I've discussed today... (to summarize)

### Ejercicio 2 — Responde apropiadamente cuando no sabes la respuesta a una pregunta

"What percentage of customers are from Europe specifically?" → ___

### Ejercicio 3 — Ordena estas frases en una secuencia lógica de presentación

1. "Thank you for your attention."
2. "Today, I'll cover three main points."
3. "Good morning, everyone."
4. "In closing, I'd like to emphasize the importance of this initiative."

### Ejercicio 4 — Corrige el registro (hazlo más apropiado para una presentación formal)

"So yeah, that's basically it. Any questions?"

### Ejercicio 5 — Repaso mezclado (interleaving: toda la Fase 5)

1. She ___ (open) her presentation ___ (by/thank) the audience, and then ___ (move on) to her main points.
2. If the presenter ___ (prepare) better, she ___ (handle) the questions more confidently.
3. Despite ___ (feel) nervous, he ___ (deliver) an excellent presentation.

### Ejercicio 6 — Producción libre

Escribe la apertura y el cierre de una presentación formal (6-8 frases en total) sobre un tema de tu elección, incluyendo al menos una transición y una respuesta a una pregunta hipotética del público.

## Solutions

### Ejercicio 1

1. **Good morning**, everyone. Thank you for **being here** today.
2. **Turning to** our next point.
3. **To summarize** the key points I've discussed today...

### Ejercicio 2

No hay una única respuesta correcta. Ejemplo válido:

> "That's a great question. I don't have that specific breakdown with me right now, but I'd be happy to follow up with you after the presentation."

### Ejercicio 3

**3, 2, 4, 1** (Good morning → Today I'll cover... → In closing... → Thank you for your attention)

### Ejercicio 4

Ejemplo de versión más formal:

> "To summarize, these are the key findings I wanted to share with you today. I'd be happy to take any questions."

### Ejercicio 5

1. She **opened** her presentation **by thanking** the audience, and then **moved on** to her main points.
2. If the presenter **had prepared** better, she **would have handled** the questions more confidently.
3. Despite **feeling** nervous, he **delivered** an excellent presentation.

### Ejercicio 6

No hay una única respuesta correcta. Ejemplo válido:

```text
Good afternoon, everyone. Thank you for being here today. I'm excited to share our proposal for the new marketing strategy.

[middle section omitted]

To summarize, we believe this strategy will significantly increase brand awareness over the next year. Thank you for your attention. I'd be happy to take any questions.

[Question]: "What's the estimated budget for this?"

That's a great question. I don't have the exact figures with me right now, but I'll send a detailed breakdown to everyone by tomorrow.
```

Revisa: ¿incluiste apertura, al menos una transición, cierre, y una respuesta apropiada a una pregunta?

## 8. 👂 Mini-listening / pronunciación

- En presentaciones formales, el ritmo suele ser más **pausado y claro** que en conversación casual, con pausas deliberadas después de puntos importantes.
- Practica leyendo en voz alta el fragmento de la sección 4, con un tono confiado y bien pausado, típico de una presentación profesional.
