## 2. Ampliando las comparaciones

### 2.1 "The same as": igual que

Ya conoces "as...as" (Unidad 011) para decir que algo es igual en una cualidad. Ahora añadimos "**the same as**", que compara cosas directamente como "iguales":

```text
My car is the same as yours. (Mi carro es igual que el tuyo.)
This restaurant is the same as the one downtown. (Este restaurante es igual que el del centro.)
```

**Diferencia con "as...as":** "as tall as" compara una cualidad específica (altura); "the same as" dice que dos cosas son básicamente iguales en general (o en algo que ya quedó claro por contexto).

### 2.2 "Different from": diferente de

```text
My job is different from my sister's job. (Mi trabajo es diferente al de mi hermana.)
This city is very different from my hometown. (Esta ciudad es muy diferente a mi ciudad natal.)
```

**Nota:** en inglés, "different from" es la forma considerada más "correcta" tradicionalmente, aunque también vas a escuchar "different than" (más común en inglés americano informal) y "different to" (más común en inglés británico). Las tres se entienden perfectamente.

### 2.3 "Less...than": menos...que

Ya conoces "more...than" (Unidad 011) para "más que". Su opuesto es "**less...than**":

```text
This book is less interesting than that one. (Este libro es menos interesante que ese.)
I have less time than before. (Tengo menos tiempo que antes.)
```

**Nota:** "less" se usa con adjetivos largos y con sustantivos incontables. Para sustantivos contables, se usa "fewer" (menos, en cantidad) — aunque en el habla informal muchos hablantes nativos usan "less" también con contables, aunque técnicamente no sea "correcto".

- Formal/correcto: "I have **fewer** books than you." (contable)
- Formal/correcto: "I have **less** money than you." (incontable)

## 3. Conectando ideas: because, but, although

### 3.1 *Because*: causa (porque)

Ya has usado "because" de forma intuitiva en unidades anteriores. Formalicemos su uso:

**Patrón:** idea 1 + because + idea 2 (la causa)

```text
I'm tired because I worked all day.
She didn't come to the party because she was sick.
```

### 3.2 *But*: contraste simple (pero)

**Patrón:** idea 1 + but + idea 2 (contraste)

```text
I like coffee, but I don't like tea.
He wanted to go, but he couldn't.
```

### 3.3 *Although*: contraste más formal (aunque)

**Although** también conecta dos ideas contrastantes, como "but", pero de una manera más formal y con una estructura ligeramente distinta: puede ir al **inicio** de la frase.

**Patrón 1:** idea 1 + although + idea 2

```text
I like my job, although it's very stressful.
```

**Patrón 2 (also correcto, empezando con although):** Although + idea 1, + idea 2

```text
Although it's very stressful, I like my job.
```

**Comparación entre but y although:**

- "I like my job, **but** it's stressful." (más simple, más común en el habla)
- "**Although** it's stressful, I like my job." (más formal, más común en escritura)

Ambas comunican la misma idea de contraste — la diferencia es de registro (formalidad) y de posición en la frase.

## 4. Ejemplo en contexto: todo junto

```text
My new job is different from my old one, but I like it more. Although
it's less relaxed than before, I have more opportunities to grow.

I have to work longer hours because we have fewer employees now.
Although it's difficult, I try to stay positive.

My salary is the same as my colleague's, but our responsibilities
are very different. She has less experience than me, although she
works just as hard.
```

Traducción de apoyo:

> Mi nuevo trabajo es diferente al anterior, pero me gusta más. Aunque
> es menos relajado que antes, tengo más oportunidades de crecer.
>
> Tengo que trabajar más horas porque tenemos menos empleados ahora.
> Aunque es difícil, trato de mantenerme positiva.
>
> Mi salario es igual al de mi colega, pero nuestras responsabilidades
> son muy diferentes. Ella tiene menos experiencia que yo, aunque
> trabaja igual de duro.

## 6. Práctica guiada (con ayuda)

**Frase 1:** Mi trabajo es diferente al de mi hermano.
Pista: my job + is + different from + ...
Respuesta: **My job is different from my brother's.**

**Frase 2:** Tengo menos tiempo que antes.
Pista: I + have + less + sustantivo incontable + than
Respuesta: **I have less time than before.**

**Frase 3:** Aunque estoy cansada, voy a terminar esto.
Pista: Although + idea 1, + idea 2
Respuesta: **Although I'm tired, I'm going to finish this.**

## 7. 🧠 Retrieval practice

### Ejercicio 1 — Completa con less o fewer

1. I have ___ money than last year.
2. There are ___ students in this class than last year.
3. She has ___ patience than her sister.
4. We need ___ ingredients for this recipe.

### Ejercicio 2 — Completa con because, but o although

1. I couldn't go to the party ___ I was sick.
2. ___ it was raining, we still went for a walk.
3. He wanted to help, ___ he didn't have time.
4. She's tired ___ she worked all night.

### Ejercicio 3 — Completa con the same as o different from

1. My opinion is ___ yours — we both agree.
2. This restaurant is completely ___ the other one — the food is nothing alike.
3. Her personality is very ___ her sister's.

### Ejercicio 4 — Une las frases usando el conector indicado

1. I like my job. It's very stressful. (although)
2. She was tired. She finished the project. (but)
3. We stayed home. It was raining. (because)

### Ejercicio 5 — Repaso mezclado (interleaving: Fase 1 y 2)

1. I ___ (have to/finish) this report, although I ___ (be) very tired.
2. She ___ (can/speak) three languages, but she ___ (can't/write) well in any of them.
3. ___ you ___ (ever/visit) a country that was very ___ (different) from yours?

### Ejercicio 6 — Producción libre

Escribe un párrafo de 5-6 frases comparando dos cosas de tu vida (dos trabajos, dos ciudades, dos etapas de tu vida), usando al menos: una comparación con "less/fewer", "the same as" o "different from", y los tres conectores (because, but, although).

## Solutions

### Ejercicio 1

1. I have **less** money than last year.
2. There are **fewer** students in this class than last year.
3. She has **less** patience than her sister.
4. We need **fewer** ingredients for this recipe.

### Ejercicio 2

1. I couldn't go to the party **because** I was sick.
2. **Although** it was raining, we still went for a walk.
3. He wanted to help, **but** he didn't have time.
4. She's tired **because** she worked all night.

### Ejercicio 3

1. My opinion is **the same as** yours — we both agree.
2. This restaurant is completely **different from** the other one — the food is nothing alike.
3. Her personality is very **different from** her sister's.

### Ejercicio 4

1. **Although I like my job, it's very stressful.** (o "I like my job, although it's very stressful.")
2. **She was tired, but she finished the project.**
3. **We stayed home because it was raining.**

### Ejercicio 5

1. I **have to finish** this report, although I **am** very tired.
2. She **can speak** three languages, but she **can't write** well in any of them.
3. **Have** you **ever visited** a country that was very **different** from yours?

### Ejercicio 6

No hay una única respuesta correcta. Ejemplo válido:

> "My old job was different from my new one. I have less free time now, but I earn more money. Although my new job is more stressful, I like it because I learn new things every day. My salary is the same as my colleague's, but my responsibilities are different."

Revisa: ¿usaste correctamente less/fewer, the same as/different from, y los tres conectores?

## 8. 👂 Mini-listening / pronunciación

- "although" se pronuncia /ɔːlˈðoʊ/ — el sonido "th" con la lengua entre los dientes, seguido de una vocal larga.
- "because" en el habla rápida a menudo se acorta a "**'cause**" (informal) — muy común en conversación casual.
- "but" es una palabra corta, casi sin énfasis, que conecta rápidamente dos ideas al hablar.
